## Quick answer

**Yes** — Roger Waters has a full 2027 tour on sale, but with one big caveat: he will not be on stage. His new production, "Roger Waters Presents Legacy – A Pink Floyd Show," was announced September 29, 2026 by AEG Presents and ICON LIVE, and runs 56 dates across North America, opening February 15, 2027 in Columbus, Georgia and closing September 25, 2027 in St. Louis, Missouri. Waters, 83, is the show's creative director; his son Harry Waters leads a 10-piece band performing Pink Floyd's 1971–1979 golden era. **October 2026 check:** European dates have been promised but not yet announced, and Roger has not said he will appear on stage at any show.

## Why a 2027 tour makes sense

Roger Waters turned 83 in September 2026, and his 2022–2023 "This Is Not a Drill" run was billed as his first farewell tour, ending in 2023. LEGACY is his answer to the question of what happens to the music after a frontman steps back: a torch-passing to the next generation, with his own son at the keyboard. Harry Waters spent more than 15 years performing alongside his father on major tours — including the Dark Side of the Moon and Wall productions and the 2016 Desert Trip concerts — and his toddler voice can be heard on "Goodbye Blue Sky" from The Wall. This is a band led by someone who has lived inside the catalog, not a tribute act learning it from the outside.

The seed of the project was public. In April 2026, Roger took to X to search for a frontman who could handle the demanding vocals of his younger days, and more than 3,000 audition submissions came in before Canadian singer Clifton David Broadbridge — now Nashville-based — was chosen after an in-person audition with Harry. That extraordinary global interest is a sign of how much demand remains for this music on stage.

The format also makes sense: this is a theater tour, not an arena run. Stops like Atlanta's Fox Theatre, Chicago's The Chicago Theatre, San Francisco's Golden Gate Theatre, the Greek Theatre in Los Angeles and San Diego's Rady Shell are intimate rooms where a note-for-note recreation of the albums — which Harry says is the explicit goal — will land with real power.
