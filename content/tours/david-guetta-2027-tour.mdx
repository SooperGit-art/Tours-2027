## Why a 2027 tour makes sense

David Guetta is coming off the biggest year of his career. In June 2026 he played a historic three-night residency at Paris' Stade de France that moved more than 240,000 tickets, one of the most ambitious productions of his career, and the aftermovie — which doubled as his announcement of the 2027 French festival run — went viral, including a moment where a Black Eyed Peas track briefly slowed at the booth and the crowd rolled with it. That same summer he appeared on the new Quasar stage at Coachella and held down his usual dual Ibiza residencies at UNVRS and Ushuaïa.

The other pillar of the 2027 story is the Monolith production itself. Built with High Scream, the creative studio behind the 2024 Paris Olympics closing ceremony, the show toured stadiums across Germany, Belgium, and Portugal over summer 2026 with custom visual architecture and heavy production elements. Guetta has said the London date will be the biggest version of the show he has brought to the city yet — and with The O2's 20th anniversary running all summer 2027, the venue framed his booking as a headline act of its celebration programme.

Musically, he arrives with two decades of catalog behind him — "Titanium," "Memories," "Sexy Bitch," "Hey Mama," "I'm Good (Blue)" — plus newer collaborations like "Beautiful People" with Sia and "Gone Gone Gone" with Teddy Swims and Tones and I. With over 80 million monthly Spotify listeners and 50 billion career streams, he remains one of the most reliably bankable live draws in dance music, which is why a full 2027 world tour announcement remains a matter of when rather than if.
