## Quick answer

**Yes** — Queens of the Stone Age are touring in 2027 on the Perfecth World Tour, supporting their ninth album *Perfecth*. The announced run so far covers 17 headlining dates: Mexico City on March 6, 2027, and a 16-date US arena leg from May 1 in San Diego through May 24 in Boston, with Pixies and Wheelchair Sports Camp supporting every US show.

**October 2026 check:** the US leg and Mexico City are confirmed and on sale this week. The band says these are the first dates of the world tour, with more shows to be announced.

## The tour so far, for context

The Perfecth World Tour follows the band's 2023 album *In Times New Roman…* and its extensive touring cycle, and it arrives with a deliberately theatrical rollout: the band debuted the *Perfecth* track "Where the Goth Girls At?" on *The Tonight Show* and is marking the album release with a costume-friendly "HalloQueen" retail campaign on Halloween. *Perfecth* is the follow-up the band's fans have waited three years for, and the 2027 routing reflects that ambition — a full US arena run rather than a festival-only summer, with a support bill that pairs two generations of alternative rock in Queens of the Stone Age and Pixies.

Before the US leg, the band's 2027 calendar already includes Mexico City and the Lollapalooza South America festivals, and the late-2026 schedule runs through Japan, Singapore, Hong Kong, Australia, and New Zealand. The pattern is a genuine world tour in stages, not a one-off US swing — which is why further legs, including Europe, are widely expected even though nothing beyond May 2027 is confirmed yet.

## Ticket and presale info

The artist presale begins Wednesday, October 28, 2026, at 10am local time via Ticketmaster, open to fans who registered by 11:59pm ET on Sunday, October 25. General sale follows Thursday, October 29, 2026, at 10am local time. As with past Queens of the Stone Age tours, tickets are sold through Ticketmaster, and the Red Rocks date (May 12) is likely to be among the fastest sellers given the venue's size and the bill.
