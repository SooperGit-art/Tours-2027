## The story behind the 2027 shows

The 2027 dates ride on the momentum of Robbie's latest album era. In January 2026 he surprised fans by releasing his 13th studio album, *BRITPOP*, weeks ahead of schedule. The album extended his record for the most UK No. 1 albums by a solo artist — a benchmark that keeps him, per *Parade*, the artist with the most UK chart-topping albums in history.

The album launched the BRITPOP World Tour, which began in summer 2025 with more than 250,000 people at his UK shows and around 1.2 million attendees across the whole run. Through 2026 the tour moved across Europe, then Central and South America (Mexico, Argentina, Brazil, Peru), before finishing the year in Australia and New Zealand — including stadium dates in Melbourne, Sydney and Auckland. Along the way he also performed at the 2026 FIFA World Cup Final in New York, which kept his global profile at its peak ahead of the 2027 announcements.

The 2027 shows were announced in July 2026. Lytham Festival called it the earliest headliner announcement in the festival's history, with co-founder Peter Taylor calling Williams "music royalty." The Irish date follows the sold-out Croke Park show of 2025 and proved so popular that a second Thomond Park night was added. The Silverstone date puts him on the Thursday opening night of the British Grand Prix weekend — the kind of one-off spectacle appearance that has become a Robbie Williams trademark.

And then there is Glastonbury. The festival returns from its 2026 fallow year on June 23–27, 2027 — the week before all four of his confirmed shows. Williams has played Worthy Farm before (a solo set in 1998, and the famous 1995 appearance with Oasis), and in 2023 he told *NME* he believed he would "slaughter it" if given the Legends Slot. Nothing has been confirmed, but the timing of his 2027 shows means speculation will keep swirling.
