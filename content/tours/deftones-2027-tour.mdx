## Why a 2027 tour makes sense

Deftones are in the middle of the biggest run of their career. Their tenth studio album, *private music*, arrived August 22, 2025 via Reprise and Warner Records, produced by Nick Raskulinecz (the man behind *Diamond Eyes* and *Koi No Yokan*) after sessions in Malibu, Joshua Tree, and Nashville. It picked up a Grammy nomination for Best Rock Album at the 68th Annual Grammys in 2026, and first single "Milk of the Madonna" put them back on rock radio in a real way. Chino Moreno told KROQ the band is "bigger than we've ever been" in their almost 30-year existence — a resurgence partly fueled by TikTok-era nü-metal nostalgia and partly just decades of influence compounding.

The touring to back it has been relentless. Deftones opened 2026 with a full European and UK arena tour spanning Paris to London's O2, followed by South American festival stops at Lollapalooza Argentina, Chile, and Brazil plus Estéreo Picnic in Bogotá and a headline arena night at Mexico City's Palacio de los Deportes. May brought Japan and Oceania dates, and the summer turned into a European festival circuit: Vienna, Berlin's Parkbühne Wuhlheide, London's All Points East x Outbreak, Dublin, Edinburgh, Paris's Rock en Seine, and Lisbon's MEO Kalorama. The band then co-headlined US stadium dates alongside System of a Down, Korn, and Avenged Sevenfold, making 2026 easily their busiest calendar in years.

The fall closes with two headline-grabbing commitments: Sick New World festival at Texas Motor Speedway in Fort Worth on October 24, and the seventh annual Dia De Los Deftones at Petco Park in San Diego on November 7 — a band-curated bill with The Neighbourhood, AFI, Converge, Jehnny Beth, Bktherula, Ladrones, Lip Critic, and LustSickPuppy. That festival has grown from a one-off hometown party into one of alternative music's most respected destination events, and its continued annual return is the closest thing Deftones have to a guaranteed date on next year's calendar.
